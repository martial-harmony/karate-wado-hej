## Important Distinctions – 重要术语辨析

这些术语容易混淆。理解它们之间的区别，有助于更准确地理解教练的指令和空手道技术。

| Term A | Term B | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| **Kihon (基本)** | **Kata (型)** | Kihon means fundamental techniques practiced individually. Kata is a structured sequence of techniques performed in a set pattern. / Kihon 是基本技术练习；Kata 是按照固定套路组合完成的动作。 |
| **Kata (型)** | **Kumite (組手)** | Kata is a formal sequence of movements, while Kumite is partner practice or sparring. / Kata 是套路练习；Kumite 是双人对练或实战练习。 |
| **Uke (受け)** | **Barai (払い)** | Uke generally means receiving or blocking an attack. Barai means sweeping or clearing an attack away. / Uke 通常指接挡；Barai 强调扫开、拨开。 |
| **Tsuki (突き)** | **Uchi (打ち)** | Tsuki generally refers to thrusting or punching techniques. Uchi refers to striking techniques. / Tsuki 通常指冲拳、直刺类技术；Uchi 指打击类技术。 |
| **Keri (蹴り)** | **Geri (蹴り)** | Both mean kicking. Geri is commonly used as the voiced form when combined with another word, such as Mae-geri. / 两者都表示踢；与其他词组合时，常发生浊音变化，例如 Mae-geri。 |
| **Oi-zuki (追い突き)** | **Gyaku-zuki (逆突き)** | Oi-zuki is a punch delivered while stepping forward with the same-side arm and leg. Gyaku-zuki is a reverse punch using the arm opposite to the forward leg. / Oi-zuki 是前进步与同侧手臂配合的冲拳；Gyaku-zuki 是与前脚相反一侧手臂打出的逆拳。 |
| **Jōdan (上段)** | **Chūdan (中段)** | Jōdan refers to the upper level, usually the head or face. Chūdan refers to the middle level, usually the torso. / Jōdan 是上段，通常指头部或面部；Chūdan 是中段，通常指躯干。 |
| **Gedan (下段)** | **Chūdan (中段)** | Gedan refers to the lower level, usually around the abdomen or below, depending on the technique. Chūdan refers to the middle level. / Gedan 是下段；Chūdan 是中段。 |
| **Kamae (構え)** | **Yōi (用意)** | Kamae means a fighting posture or guard position. Yōi means ready or prepare. / Kamae 是具体的格斗架势；Yōi 是准备就绪的指令。 |
| **Maai (間合い)** | **Hyōshi (拍子)** | Maai refers to the effective distance between opponents. Hyōshi refers to rhythm, cadence or timing. / Maai 是双方之间的攻防距离；Hyōshi 是节奏和时机。 |
| **Tai-sabaki (体捌き)** | **Ashi-sabaki (足捌き)** | Tai-sabaki refers to body movement and positioning to evade or control an attack. Ashi-sabaki refers specifically to footwork. / Tai-sabaki 是整体身体移动与闪避；Ashi-sabaki 专指步法。 |
| **Kiai (気合)** | **Kime (決め)** | Kiai is the focused shout or expression of spirit and energy. Kime is the decisive focus of power at the moment of a technique. / Kiai 是气合发声；Kime 是动作完成瞬间的集中发力。 |
| **Sensei (先生)** | **Senpai (先輩)** | Sensei is the instructor or teacher. Senpai is a more senior student or practitioner. / Sensei 是老师或教练；Senpai 是资深学员或前辈。 |
| **Rei (礼)** | **Seiza (正座)** | Rei means bowing or showing respect. Seiza is the formal kneeling sitting position. / Rei 是行礼；Seiza 是正坐姿势。 |
| **Onegaishimasu (お願いします)** | **Arigatō gozaimashita (ありがとうございました)** | Onegaishimasu is commonly said before training, expressing a polite request to train together. Arigatō gozaimashita is commonly said after training to express thanks. / Onegaishimasu 通常在训练前说，表示请多指教；Arigatō gozaimashita 通常在训练后说，表示感谢。 |

### Common Pronunciation Changes – 常见发音变化

Japanese karate terminology often combines words. When words are combined, pronunciation may change slightly.

| Base word | Combined word | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Keri (蹴り) | Mae-geri (前蹴り) | K changes to G in the compound word. |
| Tsuki (突き) | Gyaku-zuki (逆突き) | Tsuki becomes Zuki. |
| Tsuki (突き) | Oi-zuki (追い突き) | Tsuki becomes Zuki. |
| Tachi (立ち) | Kiba-dachi (騎馬立ち) | Tachi becomes Dachi. |
| Tachi (立ち) | Zenkutsu-dachi (前屈立ち) | Tachi becomes Dachi. |

**Tip for beginners:** Learn the terms together with the actual movements. In karate, the same word can have a slightly different practical meaning depending on the technique and the instructor's explanation.
