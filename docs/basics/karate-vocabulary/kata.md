A kata (型) is a fixed sequence of techniques practised alone against imaginary opponents. Wadō-ryū has 15 traditional kata: the five Pinan kata and ten others. The meanings below are the commonly given ones; interpretations differ between sources.

## Numbers Used in the Pinan Names – 平安名称中的序数

| Romaji | English | Svenska | 日本語 | 中文 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shodan | First level (1) | Första nivån (1) | 初段 | 初段 |
| Nidan | Second level (2) | Andra nivån (2) | 二段 | 二段 |
| Sandan | Third level (3) | Tredje nivån (3) | 三段 | 三段 |
| Yondan | Fourth level (4) | Fjärde nivån (4) | 四段 | 四段 |
| Godan | Fifth level (5) | Femte nivån (5) | 五段 | 五段 |

## The Pinan Series – 平安系列

The name Pinan (平安) means peace and tranquility. These five kata are usually taught first.

| Romaji | English | Svenska | 日本語 | 中文 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pinan Shodan | Peace and tranquility, level 1 | Frid och lugn, nivå 1 | 平安初段 | 平安初段 |
| Pinan Nidan | Peace and tranquility, level 2 | Frid och lugn, nivå 2 | 平安二段 | 平安二段 |
| Pinan Sandan | Peace and tranquility, level 3 | Frid och lugn, nivå 3 | 平安三段 | 平安三段 |
| Pinan Yondan | Peace and tranquility, level 4 | Frid och lugn, nivå 4 | 平安四段 | 平安四段 |
| Pinan Godan | Peace and tranquility, level 5 | Frid och lugn, nivå 5 | 平安五段 | 平安五段 |

## Notes – 使用说明

- Spellings vary between sources and organisations (for example Kushanku / Kūshankū, Seishan / Seisan, Naihanchi / Naifanchin).
- The order in which kata are taught, and which kata are required for each grade, depends on your dojo and association. Follow your instructor.
- Terms often used when studying kata, such as Bunkai (分解) and Embusen (演武線), are on the Wadō-ryū, Kata and Mindset page.
